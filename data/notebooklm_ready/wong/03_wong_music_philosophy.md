# Wong Kar-wai Music Philosophy and Soundtrack Selection
**Auteur:** Wong Kar-wai | **Category:** music | **ID:** wong_music_philosophy

---

## Music Philosophy

### Core Concept

Music as unseen character

### Function

Provides non-verbal dialogue

### Emotional Role

Crucial anchor within fragmented narratives

### Selection Timing

Chosen during pre-production, shapes overall mood


## In The Mood For Love

### Yumejis Theme

#### Composer

Shigeru Umebayashi

#### Original Film

Yumeji (1991, Seijun Suzuki)

#### Usage Count

9 times throughout film

#### Emotional Effect

Unspoken longing, unrequited love

### On Set Usage

#### Technique

Played on set during filming

#### Purpose

Imbue cast/crew with intended atmosphere

#### Influence

Shaped camerawork and visual rhythm

### Slow Motion Pairing

#### Technique

Haunting strings with slow-motion sequences

#### Effect

Amplifies emotional complexity of interactions


## Re Contextualization

### Approach

Frequently re-contextualizes pre-existing music

### Effect

Songs from different eras than film's setting

### Purpose

Creates ambiguity and complexity


## Recurring Musical Elements

### Nat King Cole

Spanish language songs in Chungking Express

### Michael Galasso

Original compositions for In the Mood for Love

### Latin Music

Dreamers' escape and romantic longing

### Pop Songs

Contemporary emotional resonance


## Emotional Anchoring

### Fragmented Narrative

Music provides coherence

### Temporal Displacement

Era-mismatched songs enhance nostalgia

### Repetition

Same theme repeated builds emotional association
