**Auteur:** Denis Villeneuve | **Category:** film_analysis | **ID:** villeneuve_dune_design

## Sandworm Design

### Development Time

Full year dedicated to design

### Design Philosophy

#### Goal

Believable prehistoric beast

#### Details

- Skin texture
- Mouth mechanics
- Feeding system in sand

### Aesthetic Vision

#### Comparison

Prehistoric whale, not just worm

#### Qualities

Ancient and majestic, god-like

### Vfx Supervisor

Paul Lambert

### Animation Approach

#### Movement

Slow but powerful

#### Sand Interaction

Crucial for conveying scale

#### Inspiration

Whales cresting through waves (sand whale effect)

#### Rejected

Snake-like or earthworm-like animations

### Physical Design

#### Structure

Rigid plates with soft accordion-like membrane

#### Effect

Allows subtle movement and tangible appearance

### Fremen Perspective

#### Perception

God-like, sacred creatures

#### Symbolism

Earth, creation, power beyond human comprehension


## Greig Fraser Cinematography

### Format Choices

#### Exteriors

Full IMAX for vastness

#### Interiors

2.39 aspect ratio

### Desert Approach

#### Lighting

Natural light wherever possible

#### Technique

Light shapers to create shadows

#### Locations

Jordan and Abu Dhabi deserts

### Camera Philosophy

#### Positions

Natural, achievable in real-world scenario

#### Creative Thought

Contemplated placing camera on another sandworm

### Part Two Additions

#### Variety

Eclipses and black sand for Arrakis landscape

#### Visual Language

Balance epic wide shots with intimate close-ups

### Spielberg Observation

Unique visual language noted


## Collaboration

### Villeneuve Storyboards

Detailed, provided strong foundation

### Vfx Principle

Good VFX during day = good lighting during filming
